Abstract
This paper bridges Jon Barwise’s Situation Semantics with continuous model theory to
address information alignment across decentralized multi-agent architectures. We demon-
strate how localized theories, containing unique vocabularies and isolated data domains,
achieve global consensus without heavy bandwidth communication over uncompressed mod-

dense topologies, identify a novel resonant loop bottleneck paradox inherent to complete
subgraph minors, and outline a structural pruning criteria optimized for parallel hardware
cores.
1 Introduction
Distributed multi-agent systems frequently operate with fragmented visibility, isolated knowl-
edge repositories, and distinct localized vocabularies. When separate agents encounter a shared
global event from disparate monitoring vantage points, establishing a unified consensus map
typically introduces severe bottlenecks. Classical symbolic AI architectures rely on heavy, ex-
haustive tree-search sweeps or the continuous broadcasting of uncompressed transactional state
configurations (models). Conversely, connectionist systems leverage dense vector operations but
frequently sacrifice structural verification and clear logical accountability.
This paper presents an integration that resolves this dichotomy by combining Jon Barwise’s
structural Channel Theory with the continuous model-theoretic paradigms of Ben-Yaacov, Hen-
son, and Zilber. Following Barwise’s core structural principle, decentralized agents optimize
communication bandwidth by exchanging compressed, localized signatures and abstract con-
straints (local logics) rather than raw transactional data databases.
We model this framework by mapping distributed agent systems directly onto a relational
graph layout. The initial phase of an unaligned operation is treated as an isolated, multi-
dimensional tensor space consisting of disjoint local models and anonymous constants. The
moment a communication bridge opens, the system initiates an infomorphism that triggers
algebraic edge contractions. In accordance with structural graph minor theory, these contrac-
tions eliminate variable redundancies and compress the data layer into an optimized graph
minor without altering or destroying the historical edge contexts of the independent entities.
To execute inference, the discrete symbolic paths are lifted into a continuous wave-propagation
field governed by a Symmetric Normalized Graph Laplacian. Instead of a sequential path-
picking or discrete token-shuffling algorithm, information propagates in parallel across the topol-
ogy as a continuous feature wave. We evaluate the performance and boundaries of this engine by
subjecting it to dense, cyclic terrains and structural obstructions, revealing unique constructive
and destructive wave superposition dynamics.
