Adaptive queuing layer and finite energy buffers

The queue for our example scenario is two servers, SG and PV, and one consumer the EV. 
Figure 1: queuing diagram: 2 circles vertically on the right, one queue represented as a rectangle, open on the left, to the left of the servers. One arrow into the queue, and 1 arrows out of each server.
Scenario #1:  a GFM has a power source, PV, and it's sending power to a battery/GFL. Then somebody, say a GFL with a lot of EV chargers, needs some battery power. Also, the AGC decides when to charge the batteries from the SG or PV.
	The normal queuing theory applies, but the queue, representing the stored charge SOC, is not only finite, but it varies.
Not to worry, we will perform congestion control (AGC) on the queue to keep it approximately fixed in size, say 80% full capacity.
Remark 1: The ability to control SG by AGC is one reason why it’s better to use batteries in the Power Company rather than batteries by a Data Center.
	We will use a Machine Learning (ML) scheme to provide the performance of service throughput vs demand arrival rate.
The ML allows us to adapt to changes in the power grid (the network).
We will use the extensive work in computer network performance to construct the controller.
	Training will use DES and AGC integrated with the EMT simulator.
	Adjusting the ML will use the real world data (Caltech dataset) digital twin.
The strategy is to build  Graph with all the necessary features of the traffic in and out of the queue.
A GNN will be used for per-flow mean delay prediction (power flow delay can be positive or negative battery usage).

Traffic can be of various patterns and characteristics,
often modeled as random variables (RVs). In our scenario, we
utilize an on/off pattern that keeps switching between ‘on’
(when data are generated) and ‘off’ (when the generation is
paused) phases. For each flow, the ‘on’ and ‘off’ durations
are sampled from random distributions upon switches, i.e.,
ton,i,j∼T(τon,i) and toff,i,j∼T(τoff,i) for the ith flow’s jth
on and off switches, respectively.
