\begin{abstract}
AI data centers executing distributed training workloads impose periodic, spatially correlated active-power disturbances on transmission grids. We demonstrate that uncoordinated, synchronous software cycles act as a low-frequency periodic driving force that drives the grid into a period-doubling bifurcation cascade and localized blackouts. We present a decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework that discovers an emergent CSMA/CD protocol at the grid interface boundary, restoring global phase-locking and stability without centralized scheduling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}
Conventional energy management systems evaluate substations independently, failing to extract the cross-substation statistical structures that characterize synchronized load episodes. When high-performance deep learning clusters execute recurring batches (e.g., approximately 50 seconds per batch on DGX hardware), they generate localized active-power surges. To model this interaction, we append the aggregate data center load $P_{\text{AI}, i}(t)$ directly into the second-order Kuramoto swing equations for a grid node $i$:
\begin{equation}
m_i \ddot{\theta}_i + d_i \dot{\theta}_i = P_i - \sum_{j=1}^{N} K_{ij} \sin(\theta_i - \theta_j) - P_{\text{AI}, i}(t)
\end{equation}
where $m_i$ represents the rotational turbine inertia, $d_i$ represents the natural damping, and $K_{ij}$ defines the admittance coupling strength of the high-voltage transmission lines.

\section{Multi-Facility Scaling and Normalized Sensing}
As the architecture scales up to 10,000 training nodes distributed across multiple locations at a density of 50 training nodes per facility, static global threshold sensing breaks down. The MARL agents adapt by implementing a normalized carrier-sensing metric $\overline{L}_i(t)$ tracking local facility capacity perimeters:
\begin{equation}
\overline{L}_i(t) = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} P_{\text{AI}, j}(t)}{\text{Capacity}_{\text{facility}}}
\end{equation}
This multi-scale localization allows individual clusters to sense the aggregate load acceleration ($\frac{dL}{dt}$) and execute independent out-of-phase gating delays, preventing the formation of chaotic attractors.

\section{The Core MARL Framework and Emergent CSMA/CD}
The decentralized controllers evaluate their state-action parameters using a unified, non-linear quadratic collective reward function:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{collective}}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \text{Compute\_Progress}_i(t) - \beta \cdot \max\left(0, \, L(t) - L_{\text{threshold}}\right)^2
\end{equation}
where $\beta = 1000$. Sensing an imminent collision via the load derivative, the agent introduces a randomized temporal delay gate that shifts the periodic square waves out of phase. The grid's natural damping $d_i$ and mechanical inertia $m_i$ recover dominance, dampening the frequency deviations $\dot{\theta}_i(t)$ and pulling the oscillators back into global synchronization.
