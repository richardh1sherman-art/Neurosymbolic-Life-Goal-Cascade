\section{The Mesarovic--Takahara Systems Framework}
For large-scale fleet deployments ($N=50$), complete point-to-point state-space tracking becomes computationally intractable. We address this by formalizing the system under a Mesarovic--Takahara input-output abstract response mapping:
\begin{equation}
S: M \times U \to Y
\end{equation}
where $U$ is the Disturbance Set generated by the non-linear Navier-Stokes weather advection field, $M$ is the Control Input Set of discrete structural graph interventions executed by the LISP DSL controller, and $Y$ is the System Outcome Space tracking the layered physical, communication, and computational graph networks ($\mathcal{G}_P, \mathcal{G}_C, \mathcal{G}_{\text{Comp}}$).

Because deterministic controllability fails when communication edges snap over a 100-mile domain, controllability is evaluated over a Performance Target Subset $V' \subset V$. The hypergraph system is completely controllable relative to the performance target if and only if:
\begin{equation}
(\forall u) \Big[ u \in U \to (\exists m) \big( g(m, u) \in V' \big) \Big]
\end{equation}
This establishes that for every accidental environmental breakdown ($u$), there exists a structural graph modification ($m$) that keeps the operational service yield inside the safe, high-performing zone.

\subsection{The Forbidden Failure Minor ($\mathcal{M}_{\text{danger}}$)}
The system's control boundary is strictly governed by the exclusion of the Forbidden Minor $\mathcal{M}_{\text{danger}}$. We partition the vertex set into active assets $\mathcal{V}_F$ and fuel/power depleted assets $\mathcal{V}_D$. The forbidden minor emerges if the contraction of active communication links leaves an isolated or disconnected topology within the dead node cluster:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{\text{danger}} = \mathcal{G} \setminus \mathcal{V}_F \Big/ \{ (v_i, v_j) \in \mathcal{E} \mid v_i, v_j \in \mathcal{V}_D \}
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{M}_{\text{danger}} \prec \mathcal{G}$, the algebraic connectivity collapses to zero, signaling an irreversible network split.
