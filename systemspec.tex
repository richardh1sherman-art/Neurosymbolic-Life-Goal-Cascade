\section{Second-Order Kuramoto Grid Physics and Decentralized CSMA/CD Access}
We formalize the active stabilization of shared electrical transmission networks under massive distributed training workloads as a closed-loop system mapping discrete multi-agent reinforcement learning to continuous swing equations.

\subsection{The Continuous Route to Chaos Swing Matrix}
Let $N$ represent the total number of synchronized training nodes scaling asymptotically up to $10,000$. Each electrical substation bus $i$ behaves as a coupled non-linear oscillator with an instantaneous phase angle $\theta_i(t)$ and a local phase velocity deviation $\dot{\theta}_i(t)$. The physical grid dynamics under the impact of periodic data center loads are governed by the second-order Kuramoto swing equation:
\begin{equation}
m_i \ddot{\theta}_i + d_i \dot{\theta}_i = P_i - \sum_{j=1}^{N} K_{ij} \sin(\theta_i - \theta_j) - P_{\text{AI}, i}(t)
\end{equation}
where $m_i$ represents the rotational turbine inertia, $d_i$ represents the natural damping, and $K_{ij}$ defines the admittance coupling strength of the high-voltage transmission lines. The term $P_{\text{AI}, i}(t)$ represents the aggregate load injection of the AI cluster. Prior to reinforcement learning intervention, uncoordinated training sessions run in a perfectly synchronous software loop, transforming $P_{\text{AI}, i}(t)$ into a heavy low-frequency square wave pulsing at a destructive resonant frequency:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{AI}, i}(t) = \text{Square\_Wave}(\Omega_{\text{load}} \cdot t + \phi_i), \quad \Omega_{\text{load}} \approx 0.5\text{ Hz}
\end{equation}
As the amplitude of this periodic driving force scales up, the grid frequency deviations $\dot{\theta}_i(t)$ undergo a classic period-doubling bifurcation cascade, collapsing the global phase-locked limit cycle into a chaotic attractor, causing extensive phase-slipping and localized grid blackouts.

\subsection{Multi-Facility Scaling and Normalized Sensing Operators}
As the architecture scales up to 10,000 training nodes distributed across multiple locations at a density of 50 training nodes per facility, static global threshold sensing breaks down. The MARL agents adapt by implementing a normalized carrier-sensing metric $\overline{L}_i(t)$ tracking local facility capacity perimeters:
\begin{equation}
\overline{L}_i(t) = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} P_{\text{AI}, j}(t)}{\text{Capacity}_{\text{facility}}}
\end{equation}
This multi-scale localization allows individual clusters to sense the aggregate load acceleration ($\frac{dL}{dt}$) and execute independent out-of-phase gating delays, preventing the formation of localized chaotic attractors.

\subsection{Phase-Shift Stabilization Under Rigid Periodic Software Forcing}
To prevent grid collapse without intruding into the proprietary data center software stack, the MARL controller acts exclusively as an external access gatekeeper at the network boundary interface. Let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{CD}}$ represent the collision detection function tracking aggregate load derivatives ($\frac{dL}{dt}$). Upon detection of an imminent bus overlap event, the controller instantiates a randomized temporal phase-shifting gating operator $\mathcal{G}_{\Delta t}$:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{AI}, i}^{\text{gated}}(t) = \text{Square\_Wave}\Big(\Omega_{\text{load}} \cdot (t - \Delta t_{\text{random}}) + \phi_i\Big)
\end{equation}
The application of $\mathcal{G}_{\Delta t}$ introduces an artificial, out-of-band asymmetric delay that forces destructive interference across the active load profiles:
\begin{equation}
\phi_1 \neq \phi_2^{\text{gated}} \implies \int_{t}^{t+T} \left( P_{\text{AI}, 1}(t) + P_{\text{AI}, 2}^{\text{gated}}(t) \right) e^{-i\Omega_{\text{load}}t} dt \to 0
\end{equation}
By explicitly nullifying the low-frequency Fourier spectral magnitude within the critical resonant inter-area band ($0.1 - 20\text{ Hz}$), the destructive driving term is removed from the Kuramoto system manifold. 

Following the synchronized chaos boundary criteria first established in the non-linear dynamics research of \textbf{de Sousa Vieira, Sherman, et al. (1996)}, removing this periodic forcing profile allows the grid's natural damping $d_i$ and mechanical turbine inertia $m_i$ to recover immediate dominance. The frequency deviations $\dot{\theta}_i(t)$ decay exponentially, pulling the coupled non-linear oscillators back into a stable, globally synchronized phase-locked steady state ($\circ$).
